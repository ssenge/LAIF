# Quantum lower bounds for local search via vertex congestion

Written 25 Nov 2026, Theorems 4 to 6 and Cor 7 added 26 Nov 2026.

**Setting.** As in [1]: G = (V, E) is a connected undirected graph with n vertices, and f : V → ℝ is given by an oracle that answers |v, a⟩ ↦ |v, a ⊕ f(v)⟩. The task is to find a local minimum, a vertex v with f(v) ≤ f(u) for every neighbour u, with success probability at least 2/3 for every f. An all-pairs path system P = {P^{u,w}} assigns a simple path from u to w to every ordered pair (u, w), with P^{u,u} = (u). Its vertex congestion is the largest number of paths of P that contain a vertex; g denotes the least vertex congestion over all such systems. Every vertex lies on the n paths that start at it, so g ≥ n, and g ≤ n².

**Comparison.** n^{2/3}/g^{1/3} ≥ n^{3/4}/g^{1/2} holds exactly when g ≥ n^{1/2}, so for every graph, since g ≥ n. The bound of [1, Theorem 1] is above a constant only for g < n^{3/2}, and there Theorem 1 applies with δ = 1/2, so its constant does not depend on the graph in that range. Cor 2 follows from Theorem 1 with g = O(n log n) for constant-degree expanders [2, Corollary D.1]. Cor 3 follows from Theorem 1 with a path system of congestion at most 2n(D̄ + 1) ≤ 4nd, where D̄ is the mean distance (below). Theorem 1 of [1] with the same path system gives Ω(n^{1/4}/√d), which Cor 3 exceeds by the factor n^{1/12} d^{1/6}; the bound of [6] is lower by a further factor √(log n). The bound of [6] holds for every vertex-transitive graph but exceeds a constant only for d < n^{1/2}, and there Cor 3 applies with δ = 1/4 for n large enough. The congestion of a path system is, up to the paths that end at a vertex, its vertex-forwarding index; see [7] for known bounds. For vertex-transitive graphs of polylogarithmic diameter and degree, such as many Cayley graphs, Cor 3 matches the upper bound up to polylogarithmic factors. On the hypercube and on grids of dimension at least 6, the bound Θ(N^{1/3}) up to logarithmic factors is known from [3]; the lower bound technique of [8] applies to every graph that contains a product graph, which covers graphs containing such grids, among them tori of dimension at least 6, where it is stronger than Cor 3.

## Theorem 1, the proof

**Inputs.** Fix a path system P of congestion g. For L ≥ 1 the inputs are the staircases of [2]: sequences x = (x_1, …, x_{L+1}) with x_1 = 1 and x_2, …, x_{L+1} ∈ V, and the function f_x(v) = −i n − j if v lies on the walk P^{x_1,x_2} ∘ ⋯ ∘ P^{x_L,x_{L+1}}, where i is the largest index with v ∈ P^{x_i,x_{i+1}} and v is the j-th vertex of that path, and f_x(v) = dist(v, 1) otherwise. The only local minimum of f_x is x_{L+1} [2, Lemmas 6 and 7]. So it suffices to bound the queries needed to output F(x) = x_{L+1}.

**The adversary matrix.** Write x ∧ y = k if x and y agree in x_1, …, x_k and differ in x_{k+1}. Let tail_x(k) be the set of vertices of P^{x_k,x_{k+1}} ∘ ⋯ ∘ P^{x_L,x_{L+1}}, with the first occurrence of x_k removed. If x ∧ y = k, then f_x(v) ≠ f_y(v) only for v ∈ tail_x(k) ∪ tail_y(k). Put Γ[x, y] = 1/((n − 1) n^{L−k}) when x ∧ y = k and F(x) ≠ F(y), and Γ_v[x, y] = Γ[x, y] when moreover f_x(v) ≠ f_y(v). By the positive adversary bound [4], the query complexity is at least a constant times ‖Γ_G‖/max_v ‖Γ_{G,v}‖ for the restriction to any set G of inputs.

**The weights.** Let c_u(v) be the number of w with v ∈ P^{u,w}, and c(v) = Σ_u c_u(v) ≤ g. For a continuation x' of a fixed prefix ending in x_k = u, the probability that v ∈ tail_{x'}(k) is at most

q_k(u, v) = c_u(v)/(n − 1) + (L − k) c(v)/(n(n − 1)),

since the first new segment has one fixed end and each later one has two independent uniform ends. With α_k = √q_k(x_k, v), give a pair at level k the weights U = Γ α_k, V = Γ/α_k if v lies only on the tail of x, the weights U = Γ/α_k, V = Γ α_k if v lies only on the tail of y, and U = V = Γ if v lies on both. Then Γ_v[x, y]² ≤ U[x, y] V[x, y], and the bound of a matrix norm by weighted row and column sums gives ‖Γ_v‖² ≤ max_x Σ_y U · max_y Σ_x V. Each of the three kinds of pairs contributes at most α_k at level k to a row or a column sum (the second kind by q_k/α_k = α_k, the third by min(1, q_k) ≤ α_k), so

‖Γ_{G,v}‖ ≤ 3 max_{z ∈ G} Σ_k α_k(z_k, v) ≤ 3 (Φ_max + L^{3/2} √(g/(n(n − 1)))), where Φ(z, v) = Σ_k √(c_{z_k}(v)/(n − 1)).

**The good inputs.** For a uniform random input z and a fixed v, the terms of Φ(z, v) with k ≥ 2 are independent, at most 1.1 (for n ≥ 6), and by concavity of the square root have mean at most √(c(v)/(n(n − 1))) each. Their sum has mean μ ≤ L√(2g)/n, and the multiplicative tail bound for sums of independent bounded variables gives Pr[sum ≥ t] ≤ (eμ/t)^{t/1.1}. Let G be the set of inputs with Φ(z, v) ≤ t + 1.1 for every v. With L = ⌊(n²/g)^{1/3}⌋ and g ≤ n^{2−δ}, μ ≤ √2 n^{−δ/6} and L^{3/2}√(2g)/n ≤ √2, and for t > 6.6/δ the union bound over the n vertices gives Pr[z ∉ G] ≤ 1/4 for n large enough. Then ‖Γ_G‖ ≥ 1ᵀ Γ_G 1/|G| ≥ L (1 − 2 · 1/4 − 1/n) ≥ L/3, since both inputs of a pair at level k are uniform. Hence the query complexity is at least a constant times L/(t + 4), which is Ω_δ(n^{2/3}/g^{1/3}). ∎

The weight α_k depends on the milestone x_k that the two inputs share, through c_{x_k}(v). Vertices near the shared milestone get a large weight, and the rest a weight of order √(L g)/n.

## Cor 3, the path system

Let G be vertex-transitive, and let D̄ be the mean of dist(u, w) over all ordered pairs (u, w), including u = w. For every pair u ≠ w choose P^{u,w} uniformly among the shortest paths from u to w, independently. An automorphism σ maps the shortest paths from u to w bijectively onto those from σu to σw, so Pr[v ∈ P^{u,w}] = Pr[σv ∈ P^{σu,σw}], and the expected number E c(v) of paths through v is the same for every v. Summing over v gives n · E c(v) = Σ_{u,w} (dist(u, w) + 1) = n²(D̄ + 1), so E c(v) = n(D̄ + 1) ≥ n. Each c(v) is a sum of independent indicators, and the multiplicative tail bound gives Pr[c(v) ≥ 2n(D̄ + 1)] ≤ e^{−n/3}. For n ≥ 9 the union bound over the n vertices leaves a path system with g ≤ 2n(D̄ + 1) ≤ 4nd. If d ≤ n^{1−δ}/4, then g ≤ n^{2−δ}, and Theorem 1 gives Ω_δ(n^{2/3}/(nd)^{1/3}) = Ω_δ((n/d)^{1/3}). ∎

## Theorem 4, the tight bound on Ramanujan graphs of large girth

**Statement.** Let G be a connected d-regular graph with n vertices, d ≥ 3, whose adjacency eigenvalues other than ±d lie in [−2√(d−1), 2√(d−1)], and whose girth g satisfies g ≥ (2/3 + ε) log_{d−1} n for a constant ε > 0. Then the quantum query complexity of local search on G is Ω_{d,ε}(n^{1/3}). With the upper bound O(n^{1/3} d^{1/6}) of [5, Theorem 3.2] this gives Θ(n^{1/3}) for constant d. The bipartite graphs X^{p,q} of [9] (p ≠ q primes congruent to 1 mod 4 with Legendre symbol (p/q) = −1) are (p + 1)-regular Ramanujan graphs with n = q(q² − 1) vertices and girth at least 4 log_p q − log_p 4 [9]. Since n < q³, this is at least (4/3) log_p n − log_p 4, so they satisfy the hypothesis with ε = 1/2 for q large enough; here d − 1 is a prime congruent to 1 mod 4. For the non-bipartite graphs of [9] the known girth bound is 2 log_p q, about (2/3) log_p n, which does not meet the hypothesis; among the graphs of [9] the bipartite ones are those covered. The proof gives more generally Ω(T) with T = min(n^{1/3}, (d−1)^{g/2}(log n)^{−13}) on Ramanujan graphs of girth g (see the end of the proof).

**Inputs.** The staircases are non-backtracking walks with unit steps: all sequences z = (z_1, …, z_{T+1}) with z_1 = 1, z_{k+1} a neighbour of z_k, and z_{k+1} ≠ z_{k−1} for k ≥ 2. There are d(d−1)^{T−1} of them, all used with equal weight. Put f_z(v) = −max{t : z_t = v} if v lies on the walk and f_z(v) = dist(v, 1) otherwise. The endpoint z_{T+1} is the only local minimum: it is the global minimum; an on-walk vertex with last index i ≤ T has the neighbour z_{i+1}, whose last index is larger; an off-walk vertex u ≠ 1 has a neighbour closer to 1, which either lies on the walk (value below 0) or has a smaller distance. If x ∧ y = k (the walks agree in z_1, …, z_k and differ in z_{k+1}), then f_x and f_y differ only on tail_x(k) ∪ tail_y(k), where tail_x(k) = {x_{k+1}, …, x_{T+1}}.

**Adversary matrix and weights.** Put Γ[x, y] = 1/N_k when x ∧ y = k and x_{T+1} ≠ y_{T+1}, where N_k is the number of walks y with x ∧ y = k (the same for every x). Two walks of length m = T + 1 − k from z_k with different first steps end at the same vertex only if g ≤ 2m, and then with probability at most 2d/n + 4(m + 1)(d−1)^{−(m−2)/2} = o(1) by (W1) below. Summed over the levels, these collisions remove at most 2dT/n + Σ_{m≥g/2} 4(m + 1)(d−1)^{−(m−2)/2} = o(1) from each row sum, so every row sum of Γ lies between T − o(1) and T. Since N_k is the same for every x, the partners of a uniform walk at each level are uniform, and restricting to a set Good removes at most 2T|X ∖ Good| from 1ᵀΓ1, where X is the set of all walks. Hence ‖Γ_Good‖ ≥ 1ᵀΓ_Good 1/|Good| ≥ T − o(1) − 2T/4 ≥ T/3 for every set Good containing at least 3/4 of the walks. Let q_k(z, v) be the probability that a uniformly random non-backtracking continuation of z_1, …, z_k visits v within T + 1 − k steps. The partners y of x at level k are the continuations with y_{k+1} ∉ {x_{k+1}, z_{k−1}}, so a fraction at most c_d q_k of them has v in its tail, with c_d = (d−1)/(d−2) (c_d = d/(d−1) at k = 1). The weights of Theorem 1 with α_k = √(c_d q_k) on the shared prefix give ‖Γ_{Good,v}‖ ≤ 3√c_d max_{z∈Good} Φ(z, v), where Φ(z, v) = Σ_{k=1}^{T} √q_k(z, v). Hence the query complexity is at least a constant times T / max_{z∈Good, v} Φ(z, v). This step uses no property of G beyond d ≥ 3.

**Walk counts.** Let A_t(x, y) be the number of non-backtracking walks of length t from x to y. Then A_1 = A, A_2 = A² − dI, A_{t+1} = A A_t − (d−1) A_{t−1}, and A_t = (d−1)^{t/2}(U_t(X) − U_{t−2}(X)/(d−1)) with X = A/(2√(d−1)) and U_t the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind [9]. Since |U_t| ≤ t + 1 on [−1, 1], the Ramanujan condition gives ‖A_t‖ ≤ 2(t + 1)(d−1)^{t/2} on the eigenvectors other than those of ±d, and these two contribute at most 2d(d−1)^{t−1}/n to each entry. So

(W1) A_t(x, y) ≤ 2d(d−1)^{t−1}/n + 2(t + 1)(d−1)^{t/2},  (W2) Σ_{y∈S} A_t(x, y) ≤ 2d(d−1)^{t−1}|S|/n + 2(t + 1)(d−1)^{t/2}√|S|.

A non-backtracking walk on the directed edge e = (u, w) that takes t further uniform steps is at y with probability p_t(e, y) ≤ (d−1)^{−t} A_{t+1}(u, y) ≤ 2d/n + 4(t + 2)(d−1)^{−(t−1)/2}. By the girth, two different non-backtracking walks from the same directed edge with lengths a and b and the same end give a cycle of length at most a + b.

**Splitting q.** Fix ρ = ⌈5 log_{d−1} log n⌉, s = 2ρ and L = ⌈3 log_{d−1} n⌉, so g > 2s for n large. By the union bound over times, q(e, v) ≤ Σ_{t=1}^{T} p_t(e, v). For t < s there is at most one non-backtracking walk from e to v; let ℓ(e, v) be its length (∞ if there is none). Then √q_k ≤ a_k + b_k + c_k with a_k = (d−1)^{−ℓ/2} if ℓ < ρ, b_k = (d−1)^{−ℓ/2} if ρ ≤ ℓ < s (each 0 otherwise), ℓ = ℓ(e_k, v), e_k = (z_{k−1}, z_k), and c_k the square root of Σ_{s≤t≤T} p_t(e_k, v).

**The near part.** If the walk follows the short walk from e_k to v, then ℓ drops by 1. If it leaves it, then ℓ(e_{k+1}, v) ≥ s, since otherwise two walks from z_k with different first steps, both shorter than s + 1, would end at v and close a cycle shorter than g. So the times with ℓ < ρ form runs along which ℓ decreases by 1, and each run contributes at most Σ_{j≥0} (d−1)^{−j/2} = C_d (C_3 < 3.42). Here e_k is defined for k ≥ 2, and the term k = 1 is bounded by 1. Suppose two runs for the same v, the first left at time k at w = z_k (or ended at v), the second starting at time k′ > k. Follow z from w to z_{k′}, then the short walk of length ℓ′ < ρ from e_{k′} to v, then the remaining short walk of length ℓ < ρ from w to v in reverse. This closed walk can backtrack only at the copies of v: at w the walk z leaves the short walk, and at z_{k′} the short walk starts from e_{k′}. If it contained no cycle, it would trace a tree, and at its vertex farthest from v it would backtrack, which is impossible since that vertex is not v. So it contains a cycle of length at most (k′ − k) + ℓ + ℓ′, hence k′ − k ≥ g − 2ρ + 2, while dist(z_k, z_{k′}) < 2ρ. By (W2) with S the ball of radius 2ρ, the probability that such a pair of times exists is at most T Σ_{t≥g−2ρ} (2d|S|/n + 4(t + 2)(d−1)^{−(t−1)/2}√|S|), which is O(T²(log n)^{10}/n + T g (log n)^{10}(d−1)^{−g/2}) since |S| ≤ d(d−1)^{2ρ} and (d−1)^{ρ} ≤ (d−1)(log n)^5. This is o(1) for T ≤ min(n^{1/3}, (d−1)^{g/2}(log n)^{−13}). Outside this event, Σ_k a_k ≤ C_d for every v.

**The middle and late parts.** Here b_k ≤ (d−1)^{−ρ/2} ≤ (log n)^{−5/2}, and c_k ≤ (2dT/n + O(s(d−1)^{−s/2}))^{1/2} ≤ (log n)^{−2.4} for n large. Under the uniform law π on directed edges, E_π b ≤ Σ_{ℓ<s} (d−1)^{ℓ/2}/n = (log n)^{O(1)}/n, since at most d(d−1)^ℓ of the nd directed edges have a walk of length ℓ to v, and E_π c ≤ √(T/n) by concavity, since π is stationary and its head is uniform. By the bound p_t(e, f) ≤ 2d/((d−1)²n) + 2(t + 1)(d−1)^{−(t+1)/2} for directed edges, the law of e_{k+L} given e_1, …, e_k is at most 6π for n ≥ n_0(d) (only this upper bound is used, so a bipartite G, where the walk is periodic, is covered). Split the times into their L residue classes modulo L. Along one class the conditional means of the terms sum to at most 6T√(T/n)/L = 6/L for c (at T = n^{1/3}) and to (log n)^{O(1)} n^{−2/3} for b. The multiplicative Chernoff bound for nonnegative terms at most β with conditional means summing to at most μ, Pr[S ≥ a] ≤ (eμ/a)^{a/β}, with a = 30/L for c and a = 1/L for b, gives failure probability exp(−Ω((log n)^{1.4})) per class and vertex. The first L terms add at most L((log n)^{−5/2} + (log n)^{−2.4}) = o(1). By the union bound over the L classes and the n vertices, with probability 1 − o(1) we have Σ_k (b_k + c_k) ≤ 32 for every v.

**Conclusion.** Let Good be the walks outside both events. Then Φ(z, v) ≤ 1 + C_d + 32 for every z ∈ Good and every v (the term k = 1 is at most 1), and the query complexity is Ω(T). The girth enters through g > 2s (unique short walks, the deviation step), through g → ∞ (collisions of endpoints) and through the bound on two runs, which needs T ≤ (d−1)^{g/2}(log n)^{−13}; in that range g ≥ 26 log_{d−1} log n > 2s. Under the hypothesis of the theorem T = ⌊n^{1/3}⌋ is allowed, which gives Ω(n^{1/3}). ∎

The walks are non-backtracking because a simple random walk stays near a vertex for about log T steps; for simple random walks the corresponding sum grows with n (`sim/phi_nb.py`).

## Theorem 5, the general form

**Statement.** Let G be a connected d-regular graph with n vertices, d ≥ 3, whose adjacency eigenvalues other than ±d have absolute value at most λ < d. Put λ_* = max(√(d−1), (λ + √(max(λ² − 4(d−1), 0)))/2) and θ = λ_*/(d−1), so θ < 1. If the girth g satisfies g ≥ (1/3 + ε) log_{1/θ} n for a constant ε > 0, then the quantum query complexity of local search on G is Ω_{d,λ,ε}(n^{1/3}), hence Θ(n^{1/3}) for constant d by [5, Theorem 3.2]. For every girth, the bound is Ω(T) with T = min(n^{1/3}, θ^{−g}(log n)^{−13}). For Ramanujan graphs, θ = (d−1)^{−1/2} and log_{1/θ} n = 2 log_{d−1} n, which is Theorem 4. **Scope.** The girth of a d-regular graph is at most 2 log_{d−1} n + O(1), so the hypothesis can hold only if θ ≤ (d−1)^{−1/6−ε/2+o(1)}: it asks for girth close to this bound together with a spectral bound close to the Ramanujan one. The families with an explicit λ known to us to meet it are the Ramanujan graphs of Theorem 4. The girth of a random d-regular graph stays bounded in probability, since the numbers of short cycles converge to Poisson laws with means (d−1)^k/(2k); so random d-regular graphs fail the girth hypothesis with probability 1 − o(1). Theorem 6 covers them.

**Proof.** The proof of Theorem 4 goes through with (d−1)^{−1/2} replaced by θ in the walk counts. For an eigenvalue μ with 2√(d−1) < |μ| ≤ λ, write |μ| = 2√(d−1) cosh φ; then |U_t(μ/(2√(d−1)))| = sinh((t+1)φ)/sinh φ ≤ (t + 1)e^{tφ}, and √(d−1) e^{φ} is the larger root of x² − |μ|x + (d−1), at most λ_*. So

(W1′) A_t(x, y) ≤ 2d(d−1)^{t−1}/n + 2(t + 1)λ_*^t,

and (W2) holds with λ_*^t in place of (d−1)^{t/2}. A walk on a directed edge is then at y after t further steps with probability at most 2d/n + 4d(t + 2)θ^t. The scales become ρ = ⌈5 log_{d−1} log n⌉, s = max(2ρ, ⌈6 log_{1/θ} log n⌉) and L = ⌈2 log_{1/θ} n⌉; all are O(log n), and g > 2s for n large. The middle part does not use the spectrum. The late part needs Σ_{t≥s} (t + 2)θ^t ≤ (log n)^{−5}, which holds by the choice of s. The block step needs p_L ≤ 6π, which holds by the choice of L for n large. In the near part, the probability of two runs for the same vertex is at most (log n)^{O(1)}(T²/n + Tθ^{g}). Since θ ≥ (d−1)^{−1/2}, the factors from the ball are again at most (log n)^{10}, and the bound is o(1) when T ≤ n^{1/3} and θ^{−g} ≥ T(log n)^{13}, which the girth condition gives at T = ⌊n^{1/3}⌋; in this range g ≥ 13 log_{1/θ} log n > 2s. Two branches of length m ≥ g/2 end at the same vertex with probability at most 2d/n + 4d(m + 2)θ^m = o(1). ∎

`sim/check_nb_gap.py` asserts (W1′) for t ≤ 40 on five regular graphs (random 3- and 4-regular graphs, a torus, and two graphs with λ > 2√(d−1), a prism and a circulant), with a mutation (λ_* replaced by √(d−1)) that fails on a graph with λ > 2√(d−1).

## Theorem 6 and Cor 7, random regular graphs

**Theorem 6.** Let G be a connected d-regular graph with n vertices, d ≥ 3, whose adjacency eigenvalues other than ±d have absolute value at most λ < d, and let θ and s be as in Theorem 5. Let T ≤ n^{1/3}, and suppose that with probability at least 7/8 over a uniform non-backtracking walk z of length T from vertex 1, the subgraph induced by the union of the balls of radius s around z_1, …, z_{T+1} is a forest. Then for n ≥ n_0(d, λ) the quantum query complexity of local search on G is Ω(T). The hypothesis needs about (T + 1)(d−2)(d−1)^{s−1} ≤ n, since otherwise the balls cannot be disjoint trees; with the scales of Theorem 5 it can hold only for n above roughly 10^{20} to 10^{30} (larger at T = n^{1/3}), so Theorem 6 is an asymptotic statement.

**Proof.** Take the inputs, the adversary matrix and the weights of Theorem 4 with the scales of Theorem 5, and let Good consist of the walks with this forest property for which the two Chernoff events of Theorem 4 hold; Good contains at least 3/4 of the walks for n large. The row-sum bound of Theorem 4 is used only for rows in Good: by the steps below every x ∈ Good has row sum at least T − o(1), every column sum is at most T, and so ‖Γ_Good‖ ≥ (1/|Good|) Σ_{x∈Good} (row(x) − Σ_{y∉Good} Γ[x, y]) ≥ T − o(1) − T|X ∖ Good|/|Good| ≥ 2T/3 − o(1). Every step of Theorem 4 that uses the girth looks only inside this forest. A non-backtracking walk of length less than s from e_k stays in the ball of radius s around z_k, so the short walk from e_k to v is unique and the deviation step holds. In the two-run argument the closed walk lies in the forest, so it cannot contain a cycle, and two runs for the same vertex are impossible; no bound on returns is needed. Two branches of length m < s from z_k stay in the same ball and cannot meet, and for m ≥ s they meet with probability at most 2d/n + 4d(m + 1)θ^{m−1} (a branch takes m − 1 uniform steps after its first edge), which sums to o(1) over the levels. In the middle part, b(e) is defined for every directed edge with the shortest walk of length ℓ_min(e, v) in [ρ, s), so the count E_π b ≤ Σ_{ℓ<s} (d−1)^{ℓ/2}/n needs no structure, and on Good it dominates the true term. The late part and the block step use only the spectrum, as in Theorem 5. ∎

**Cor 7.** For fixed d ≥ 3, a uniformly random d-regular graph on n vertices (nd even) has, with probability 1 − o(1), quantum query complexity Θ(n^{1/3}) for local search.

**Proof.** With probability 1 − o(1) the nontrivial eigenvalues have absolute value at most 2√(d−1) + 0.01 [10], in the form of [12, Theorem 1], which states max(μ₂, |μ_n|) < 2√(d−1) + ε with probability tending to 1 for the uniform distribution on d-regular graphs, so θ < 1 depends only on d; this also makes the graph connected and non-bipartite. For the forest property, work in the configuration model [11] and reveal the graph together with the walk: first all edges at the vertices within distance s of z_1, then the next step of the walk, which is uniform among the d − 1 forward half-edges at the current vertex, then the edges at the new vertices within distance s of z_2, and so on. Every new pairing joins a half-edge to a uniform unpaired half-edge, whatever was revealed before. Each ball of radius s + 1 has at most d((d−1)^{s+1} − 1)/(d−2) ≤ d(d−1)^{s+1} vertices other than its centre, so with N = (T + 1)d(d−1)^{s+1}, at most N pairings are made and at most N revealed half-edges are unpaired at any time, and the revealed subgraph stays a forest unless some pairing hits one of these revealed unpaired half-edges (loops, multiple edges and edges between two vertices at distance exactly s count as hits), which has probability at most N²/(nd − 2N) = n^{−1/3}(log n)^{O(1)} at T = ⌊n^{1/3}⌋. By Markov's inequality over the graph, with probability 1 − o(1) the forest property fails for at most 1/8 of the walks. The uniform model is the configuration model conditioned on a simple graph, an event of probability bounded away from 0 [11], so these 1 − o(1) statements hold for it as well. Theorem 6 gives Ω(n^{1/3}), and [5, Theorem 3.2] gives O(n^{1/3}). The failure probability over the graph is at most the probability that the eigenvalue bound fails plus (e^{(d²−1)/4} + o(1)) · 8N²/(nd − 2N), where the second term, with the factor 1/Pr[simple], is n^{−1/3}(log n)^{O(1)}. ∎ The threshold n_0 of this proof is very large: with s = O(log log n) as chosen, the forest bound falls below 1/8 only for n of order 10^{200} or more for d = 3 and 4, so the statement is asymptotic.

`sim/check_h.py` tests the forest property with s = 2 on 200 walks per graph. On random 3-regular graphs it fails for 0.53, 0.235, 0.13 and 0.105 of the walks at n = 1000, 8000, 64 000 and 256 000, close to the rate n^{−1/3}; for d = 4 the constant is larger (1.0 down to 0.62 over the same range). A torus, which has girth 4, fails it on every walk. The bound of the proof uses s = O(log log n), so these sizes test the rate, not the constant. `sim/lps.py` builds the bipartite graphs X^{5,13} (n = 2184) and X^{5,17} (n = 4896) of [9], asserts that they are 6-regular, connected, bipartite and Ramanujan with girth 8, at least (2/3 + 1/2) log_5 n and the bound of [9], and evaluates max_v Φ at T = n^{1/3} over sampled walks (at most 1.75 and 1.90).

## Scripts

`sim/check_adv.py` builds Γ and every Γ_v exactly for random 3- and 4-regular graphs with breadth-first path systems (n ≤ 12, L ≤ 3, up to 512 inputs). It asserts that x_{L+1} is the only local minimum of every f_x, that f_x and f_y differ only on the two tails, the bound q_k for every prefix and vertex, and ‖Γ_v‖ ≤ 3 max_z Σ_k α_k(z_k, v) for every v. `sim/scaling.py` computes c_u(v) as subtree sizes of breadth-first trees on random 3-regular graphs with n ≤ 4000, and evaluates g/(n ln n), the worst Φ over sampled staircases, and the explicit bound B of the proof. At these sizes L ≤ 6 and B < 1, so the scripts check the inequalities and the growth of g and Φ, not the asymptotic advantage. `sim/transitive.py` computes, for hypercubes, tori, circulants and a cycle, the exact probability that a vertex lies on a uniform shortest path, asserts that E c(v) = n(D̄ + 1) at every vertex, draws one random shortest-path system and asserts g ≤ 2n(D̄ + 1) ≤ 4nd; the Petersen graph is included as a vertex-transitive graph that is not a Cayley graph, and the cycle lies outside the range of Cor 3. On a path graph, which is not vertex-transitive, the first assert fails, as it should. Output in `sim/out.txt`:
