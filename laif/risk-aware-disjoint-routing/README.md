# Risk-aware disjoint routing: hardness, exact classical and quantum algorithms, and a proven penalty weight for QUBO encodings

Written 16 Nov 2026.

The problem is from [1], which models resilient routing in a network as follows. An undirected graph G with link latencies c ≥ 0, a source s and two terminals t₁, t₂ are given, together with a joint failure probability p(e, f) ≥ 0 for every pair of links and a weight B ≥ 0. The task is to find internally vertex-disjoint paths P₁ from s to t₁ and P₂ from s to t₂ that minimize

    F(P₁, P₂) = c(P₁) + c(P₂) + B · Σ_{e ∈ P₁, f ∈ P₂} p(e, f).

The first two terms are the latency of the working and the backup path, and the last term is the risk that one event takes down both. In [1] the problem is solved with a QUBO formulation, and the related min-prod problem is posed: find two internally vertex-disjoint paths from s to t that minimize (|P₁| + C)(|P₂| + C) for a constant C ≥ 0, with its complexity stated as open.

## Results

**Theorem 1 (hardness).** For every rational constant C ≥ 0, the min-prod problem of [1] is NP-hard, also in the version with two terminals t₁ and t₂.

**Corollary 2.** The routing problem of [1] is NP-hard even with unit latencies and a uniform joint failure probability p(e, f) = q, for any fixed rational q > 0 and rational B > 0.

**Theorem 3 (exact classical algorithm).** An exact algorithm enumerates the first path in the order of a lower bound on F and completes each candidate optimally with one shortest path computation. It evaluates at most N candidates and makes at most N + 1 stopping tests, where N is the number of simple paths P₁ whose lower bound does not exceed the optimum, and each step takes polynomial time.

**Theorem 4 (exact quantum algorithm).** Branch-and-bound over prefixes of the first path, with a monotone bound, is accelerated by quantum branch-and-bound [2]. With integer latencies and integer values B · p(e, f), it finds an optimal pair, with bounded error, using

    Õ(√T · n^{3/2} · log c_max)

calls of the branching and bounding procedures, each with at most n − 1 bound evaluations of polynomial time. Here n is the number of vertices, T ≤ nN is the number of nodes of the search tree whose bound does not exceed the optimum, c_max bounds the cost of a feasible pair, and Õ hides polylogarithmic factors. It uses polynomial space and no quantum random access memory.

This is a quantum advantage over the classical branch-and-bound search that it accelerates: that search explores the T nodes, and the quantum algorithm needs about √T of them, a quadratic speedup in the worst case up to factors polynomial in n.

**Theorem 5 (QUBO with a proven penalty weight).** The routing problem has a QUBO with one variable per link of G − t₂ for P₁, one per link of G − t₁ for P₂, and one slack variable per path at each inner vertex of degree at least 3. The slacks are used because no quadratic penalty in the variables of the links at such a vertex alone vanishes exactly at degrees 0 and 2. With p symmetric, p(e, f) = p(f, e), any penalty weight

    A > c(E) + B · Σ_{e ≠ f, unordered} p(e, f)

is sufficient for every B ≥ 0 and every symmetric p ≥ 0: every minimizer is a feasible pair of minimum cost, together with stray cycles of latency zero that can be dropped, and none if all latencies are positive. If no feasible pair exists, the minimum exceeds the bound on the right. On the network of [1] with 5 vertices and 7 links this gives 12 or 13 qubits, depending on the source, against 24 in the formulation of [1]. A qubit-efficient reformulation was given in [7]: local rules, among them merging each inner vertex of degree 2 with its two links into one variable, reduce the instance run on hardware in [1] to 7 qubits, with a penalty weight chosen for that instance. Our contribution: a penalty weight proved sufficient for every B and every symmetric p, the proof that no penalty local to an inner vertex of degree at least 3 replaces its slack, and the detection of infeasible instances. Theorem 6 carries the penalty weight over to the encoding of [7].

**Theorem 6 (penalty weight for the reduced encoding of [7]).** We use the local rules of [7] in the following form, applied to each path in G − t₂ or G − t₁. Vertices other than the terminals with degree at most 1 are removed, repeatedly. The links through an inner vertex of degree 2 are merged into one segment with one variable. At each terminal, t first and then s, the condition "exactly one segment" is solved for one dependent segment x = 1 − Σ qᵢ, with the pair penalty Σ_{i<j} qᵢqⱼ over the other segments when there are at least two; the dependent segment is not incident to the other terminal, and it is a single link when the terminal has three or more segments; if no segment qualifies, the penalty is (Σ qᵢ − 1)². An inner vertex of degree at least 3 keeps a node variable z with penalty (deg − 2z)², unless its degree is 2ℓ with ℓ one of 0, 1, q or 1 − q, in which case ℓ is its indicator. The disjointness penalty is the sum of the products of the indicators of both paths. A dependent link can take negative values when its pair penalty is positive, so the objective is not nonnegative on all assignments, and the argument of Theorem 5 does not apply as it stands. Let

    D = max c(e) + B · Σ_{f ≠ e} p(e, f)

over the dependent links at terminals with at least three segments, with D = 0 if there are none, and U = c(E) + B Σ_{unordered e ≠ f} p(e, f) the bound of Theorem 5; then D ≤ U. With p symmetric, any penalty weight A > U + D is sufficient for every B ≥ 0 and every symmetric p ≥ 0: every minimizer is a feasible pair of minimum cost, together with stray cycles of latency zero that can be dropped, and if no feasible pair exists, the minimum exceeds U. With the pair penalties at the terminals counted twice, A > U is sufficient, at the same number of qubits. On the network of [1] the encoding has 4, 7 and 7 qubits for the sources S1, S2 and S3, against 12, 13 and 13 for Theorem 5 and 24 in [1]; the count 7 for S2 is the one reported in [7]. The rules are from [7], in the form stated here; our contribution is the penalty weight with its proof.

## Formal verification

[`lean/`](lean/) is a Lean 4 library `RiskRouting` on Mathlib with 77 main theorems, no `sorry`, and only the standard axioms (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound), as listed by [`lean/RiskRouting/Axioms.lean`](lean/RiskRouting/Axioms.lean) with its output in [`lean/axioms.txt`](lean/axioms.txt). It uses Lean v4.36.0-rc1 and Mathlib at revision d875a8de5222f62cf1c2fafc7984588adadc990a. Build with `lake exe cache get` and then `lake build` in `lean/`.

- `RiskRouting/MinProd.lean`, Theorem 1 and Corollary 2. A vertex-disjoint min-min instance with bound ℓ has a solution if and only if the gadget graph has two internally vertex-disjoint paths with (|P₁| + C)(|P₂| + C) at most the threshold, with one terminal or two (`minMin_iff_minProd`, `minMin_iff_minProd₂`); the inequalities of the proof (`paper_inequalities`); the gadget has n + 1 + n(M − 1) ≤ ⌈1 + (C + 2)²⌉(n + 1)³ vertices (`card_gadget`, `card_gadget_le`, `card_gadget_poly`); with unit latencies and p(e, f) = q the routing cost is at most K exactly when the min-prod value with C = 1/(Bq) is at most (K + C)/(Bq) (`route_unit_iff_minProd₂`). As maps on instances, each reduction keeps the answer and has the stated size bound (`reduction_minProd`, `reduction_minProd₂`, `reduction_route`).
- `RiskRouting/LineGraph.lean`, the step from edge-disjoint paths. Edge-disjoint min-min with bound ℓ in G has a solution if and only if vertex-disjoint min-min with bound ℓ + 1 has one in the line graph with a source and a target vertex added, with trails shortened to paths (`eMinMin_iff_minMin`); the line graph has at most n² + 2 vertices (`card_lineV_le`); the composed reductions from edge-disjoint min-min (`reduction_line`, `reduction_minProd_e`, `reduction_minProd₂_e`, `reduction_route_e`).
- `RiskRouting/AlgR.lean`, Theorem 3. The lower bound F(P₁, P₂) ≥ c′(P₁) + L (`lower_bound`). The loop over candidates sorted by c′, which stops once the best pair found costs at most c′ + L, returns a pair of minimum cost, or none if no pair is feasible (`run_best`, `run_answer`, `algR_exact`); it evaluates at most N candidates and makes at most N + 1 stopping tests, ties included (`run_count`).
- `RiskRouting/BranchBound.lean`, Theorem 4. The children and leaves of the search tree over prefixes of P₁ (`join`, `cont_of_cand`, `cont_child`, `node_child`); the bound c′(π) + L + δ, with δ the distance of the cheapest continuation, is monotone along the tree and below every leaf, and pruning at δ = ∞ loses no leaf (`bound_mono`, `bound_le_leaf`, `prune_ok`); a node of bound at most the optimum is a prefix of a candidate with c′ + L at most the optimum, so T ≤ nN (`extend_of_bound`, `card_good_le`, `cand_length`); splitting the children of a node by dyadic index blocks gives a binary tree with at most (1 + D)T nodes of cost at most the optimum (`bcost_zero`, `block_succ`, `bcost_mono`, `parent_le_root`, `card_good_blocks`, `card_binarized`); with the guarantee of [2] as the hypothesis `QBBGuarantee`, the number of queries is at most K√((1 + D)nN) ((n − 1) max(1, D))^{3/2} log₂ c_max times the polylogarithmic factor of [2], for c_max ≥ 2 (`query_bound`).
- `RiskRouting/QUBO.lean`, Theorem 5. The penalty terms vanish exactly on the allowed local configurations (`slack_pen_zero_iff`, `pair_pen_zero_iff`, `end_pen_zero_iff`, `disj_pen_zero_iff`, with `quad_eq_eval`); at a vertex with at least three link variables no quadratic penalty without a slack works (`no_quadratic_parity`); the objective of a feasible pair is at most the bound U on the right of the condition (`obj_le_U`). For A > U every minimizer has penalty zero and yields a feasible pair of minimum cost, it is itself feasible when every infeasible assignment of penalty zero can be strictly improved, as with positive latencies, and without a feasible pair the minimum exceeds U (`qubo_minimizer`, `qubo_minimizer_strict`, `qubo_infeasible`).
- `RiskRouting/Combined.lean`, Theorem 6. If every assignment of nonzero penalty has H > U, minimizers are optimal and infeasible instances show min H > U (`minimizer_of_gap`, `infeasible_of_gap`). An objective of at least −D times the total negative part, with the negative part at most the penalty, gives that gap for A > U + D (`gap_of_neg`, `reduced_minimizer`, `reduced_infeasible`); with the negative part at most half the penalty and D ≤ U it does so for A > U (`gap_of_neg_double`, `reduced_minimizer_double`, `reduced_infeasible_double`). For the dependent link 1 − Σ qᵢ, the pair penalty is C(k, 2) for k active variables, it bounds the negative part, and a zero pair penalty leaves the link at 0 or 1 (`sum_eq_card_active`, `sum_pairs_eq_choose`, `pred_le_choose_two`, `neg_le_choose`, `dep_neg_le_pairs`, `dep_le_one_of_pairs_zero`). With link values at most 1 the objective is at least −D times the total negative part, and c(e) + B Σ_f p(e, f) ≤ U for symmetric p with zero diagonal (`mul_ge_neg_parts`, `obj_lower`, `obj_ge_neg_D`, `link_weight_le_U`). The statements take the objective, the penalty and the total negative part as given functions on the assignments.
- `RiskRouting/Decoding.lean`, Theorem 6. Zero penalty: a zero pair penalty leaves the dependent link at 0 or 1, the dependent link or the penalty (Σ − 1)² gives degree 1 at a terminal, a zero node penalty gives degree 2z, every inner vertex that is removed, merged or of degree 2ι with ι ∈ {0, 1} then has degree 0 or 2, and a zero disjointness penalty over 0/1 indicators leaves no inner vertex on both paths (`dep_value_01`, `term_sum_of_dep`, `onehot_sum`, `node_of_pen_zero`, `decode_path`, `decode_disjoint`, `decode_pair`). Conversely, a 0/1 link set with degree 1 at a terminal gives the dependent value and a zero pair penalty there, and degrees 0 or 2 give equal values at a merged vertex and zero values at a removed one (`complete_terminal`, `complete_onehot`, `complete_merge`, `complete_prune`).

**Assuming.** The NP-hardness of min-min disjoint paths with edge-disjoint paths in undirected graphs [4], used on paper together with the reductions above. The guarantee of quantum branch-and-bound [2], main theorem, stated as `QBBGuarantee`. The enumeration of simple paths in order of cost [5], which enters as the hypothesis that the candidates are sorted by c′. The shortest path step [6], which enters as the value of a candidate (the cost of its best partner) and as the distance δ of a node. The decomposition of a link set whose inner vertices have degree 0 or 2 and whose ends have degree 1 into the two paths and disjoint cycles, which enters as the hypothesis `hred` of `qubo_minimizer` and of the statements of `Combined.lean`.

**Not formalized.** That the constructions of the reductions, the algorithm of Theorem 3 and the oracles of Theorem 4 run in polynomial time; the reversibility and the space of the quantum algorithm; the assembly of the objective and the penalty of Theorems 5 and 6 from the graph, with the facts that only the dependent links counted in D take negative values and that the local terms sum to the penalty, proved on paper and checked by exhaustive search on the instances above; the qubit counts on the network of [1], checked by script.
